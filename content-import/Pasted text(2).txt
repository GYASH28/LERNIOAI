Operating as a premier autonomous institution under the Maharashtra State Board of Technical Education (MSBTE) framework since 1985, CWIT has continuously evolved its pedagogical frameworks to meet shifting industry paradigms. This report specifically focuses on the integration of high-quality digital learning resources into the Lernio platform for two distinct, forward-looking academic branches: Computer Engineering (Programme Code: CP) and Computer Engineering & Internet of Things (IoT) (Programme Code: CI).The fundamental objective of this analysis is to dissect the institution's newly implemented Multi-Point Entry Credit System (MPECS) R23 scheme, which was instituted for the academic year 2023-24 in strict alignment with the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020. The transition from the legacy MPECS 2018 (R18) and 2014 curricula represents a significant shift toward Outcome-Based Education (OBE), mandating that theoretical instruction be tightly coupled with practical, laboratory-based learning and observable cognitive outcomes. This report evaluates all six academic semesters, mapping the transition from foundational applied sciences in the initial terms to highly specialized computing modules—such as Embedded Operating Systems and Digital Techniques—in the latter stages.A critical analytical finding of this research is the deliberate structural divergence between the two branches beginning in the second semester. While the first semester exhibits a complete theoretical and practical overlap designed to establish a baseline in mathematics, physics, chemistry, and basic communication skills , the second semester introduces vital hardware paradigms specifically tailored for the CI branch. The inclusion of "Basic Electrical and Electronics Engineering" (R23CI1301) for CI students prepares them for advanced embedded systems and sensor integrations, whereas CP students proceed along a more traditional, software-centric trajectory.The integration of educational video tutorials into the Lernio platform necessitates a rigorous, syllabus-aligned mapping of these subjects. However, in strict adherence to data integrity protocols that prohibit the fabrication or hallucination of digital Uniform Resource Locators (URLs), specific video recommendations have been structurally outlined rather than populated with unverified links. CWIT operates under the Multi Point Entry Credit System (MPECS), an Outcome-Based Education (OBE) model mandated for accreditation by the National Board of Accreditation (NBA) under the Washington Accord.The curriculum is structured around 16-week semesters, requiring students to earn a total of 191 credits to obtain their Diploma. A single credit equates to one hour of theoretical lecture or one hour of practical laboratory work per week. The overarching ratio of theory to practical instruction is maintained at approximately 55:45, emphasizing the institution's commitment to hands-on engineering competencies. Furthermore, the curriculum classifies courses into five distinct categories: Foundation (Category 1), Allied (Category 2), Core (Category 3), Applied (Category 4), and Specialized (Category 5).This systematic classification is embedded directly into the institution's alphanumeric course coding matrix. Every course features a unique 9-digit identifier. For theoretical components (FA-TH), students undergo continuous evaluation through class tests, while practical competencies (FA-PR) are evaluated continuously in the laboratories. If a candidate fails to secure minimum passing marks in the practical formative assessment, they are declared "Detained" for the semester. The first semester exhibits a 100% pedagogical and structural overlap. Both the Computer Engineering (CP) and Computer Engineering & IoT (CI) branches operate under an identical Notional Learning Hour framework. The weekly schedule necessitates 15 contact hours of Classroom Learning (CL), 2 hours of Tutorial Learning (TL), and 15 hours of Laboratory Learning (LL), yielding a total of 40 Notional Learning Hours and 20 Credits. The subject matrices are entirely identical in content, encompassing Basic Mathematics, Basic Science (Physics & Chemistry), Communication Skills (English), Engineering Graphics, Engineering Workshop Practice, Fundamentals of ICT, and Yoga and Meditation.Notably, despite the identical contact hours, credit allocations, and assessment parameters (amounting to 850 total marks per semester for both branches), the institution administratively assigns distinct course codes to maintain branch identity. For example, Basic Mathematics is coded as R23CP1701 for the Computer branch and R23CI1701 for the IoT branch. From the perspective of Lernio platform integration, this means that the curated video tutorials, generated PDF notes, and quiz metadata for Semester 1 can be developed once and universally mapped across both branch databases, ensuring high resource efficiency.Second Semester Divergence:
The structural overlap begins to diverge significantly in the second semester. While both branches continue to share foundational computing concepts—specifically "Applied Mathematics," "Programming in C," "Linux Basics," and "Web Page Designing"—the CI branch introduces a vital hardware-oriented subject: "Basic Electrical and Electronics Engineering" (R23CI1301). This 5-credit Ability Enhancement Course (AEC) acts as the fundamental building block for subsequent Internet of Things (IoT) hardware interfaces. The CP branch does not carry this specific hardware requirement, focusing its available notional hours on purely software-oriented paradigms and life skills.Advanced Semester Convergence and Specialization:
In the advanced semesters (Semesters 3 through 6), both branches converge on core software paradigms that are universally essential for any computing professional. Data extracted from institutional examination schedules indicates that subjects such as "Data Structures" (R23CP2402 / R23CI2602), "Object Oriented Programming with C++" (R23CP6404 / R23CI6604), and "Software Engineering" (R23CP2409 / R23CI2610) are shared pillars across both programmes. However, the institutional coding remains strictly segregated. This structural segregation implies that while the theoretical core is shared, the practical laboratory assignments may diverge. CI students are likely to implement data structures or software architectures geared toward embedded systems and sensor arrays, whereas CP students will focus on standard desktop or enterprise software environments. Lernio's educational resources must account for this by providing a blend of theoretical algorithms and varied practical applications.Section 5 - Computer Engineering (CP) Curriculum AnalysisThe Computer Engineering (CP) curriculum is meticulously designed to develop highly proficient software developers, database administrators, and systems analysts.
